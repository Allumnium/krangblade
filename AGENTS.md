## Interface and visual design
- Minimal, consistent dark sci-fi UI. No unnecessary headings, explanations, duplicate data, filler text, default/normal modifiers, or decorative text. Prefer familiar icons and concise numbers, with exact values and optional details in tooltips.
- Use "star system" (or system) for the gameplay node and "shipyard" in all player-facing wording, never "yard". Procedurally named systems are unique and seed-stable; visible orbiting planets are decorative and not separate gameplay entities.
- Top HUD is one row: empire resources, population, year and Menu. Resource tiles fill using stored inventory relative to the provisional 1,000-unit per-owned-system reference; cargo remains included in numeric empire holdings, not storage fill. No hard storage cap yet. No game title, worlds/craft counter, reset button. Menu opens a themed modal before leaving; opening it does not pause the simulation.
- Selected system's left panel shows current population only, non-default production modifiers as clear percentages, six local stock/level/annual output rows, and persistent shipyard cards (including construction and idle states). No population growth rate, saturation bar, carrying-capacity numbers, default manufacturing multiplier, or field count. Economy tab is reserved for future manufacturing.
- Never let numeric values or resource labels wrap vertically. Use non-wrapping aligned labels, sensible minimum widths, compact K/M/B formatting for large amounts, exact tooltips, and scroll containers where appropriate. Test actual label sizes/line counts at normal and constrained window sizes; visually inspect screenshots when available. Apply a discovered layout fix to equivalent panels rather than waiting for repeated reports.
- Trade tab shows six independent dual-handle percentage sliders with resource-colored live inventory fill; no resource dropdown. Preserve dragging across snapshots. The current 1,000-unit reference is provisional, not an enforced storage limit.
- Build and upgrade actions belong on their own cards, with icon-only repeat controls, pending build counts in parentheses, and accessible cancellation. No separate queue panel or five-order limit. Canceling an order refunds allocated materials and turns off the matching repeat flag. Preserve other pending orders; cancel newest matching hull first unless directed otherwise. Repeat hull orders cycle in queue order rather than skipping a resource-starved turn; multiple shipyards can take consecutive turns concurrently. Keep manual scheduling and independent upgrade work intact. Upgrades wait their turn at the relevant shipyard and have substantial increasing material costs. Maximum three completed plus pending shipyards per system; enforce at the authority and disable the build button at the limit.
- Fog of war is currently off by default; retain discovery machinery for a future match setting. Asteroid fields have varied shapes and specialized ore mixes, individually positioned finite chunks, and resource-specific mining preferences (all enabled by default). Mining stays at least five game years after arrival. No unapproved regeneration or combat mechanics.

## Design scope
- Low-micro networked territory-control RTS. Prioritize autonomous logistics and meaningful high-level decisions. Physical ships carry resources between local stocks; local thresholds govern trade. Keep current gameplay mechanics intact when making presentation-only changes.
- Discuss unresolved design choices before inventing substantial new mechanics, but use sensible implementation defaults for clearly approved changes. Identify provisional balance and design omissions rather than treating them as settled.
- Keep README/current code authoritative for detailed implementation and tests; update these instructions when a later decision supersedes them.

- Military outposts are free declarations on any discovered neutral system (no adjacency requirement; disconnected objectives are dormant until linked to the patrol network), not buildings or colonies. Unowned-system interactions belong on the right instead of owner-only tabs. Fighter selection gives fleet-type patrol/frontier distribution. Top-center mutually exclusive military and logistics lens toggles work regardless of selection; military marks declared objectives/frontiers including unconnected outposts. Logistics shows local stock bars and zoomed quantities. Keep frontier distributions at 100%; host validates and assigns hulls. Logistics lens metrics remain provisional; combat damage is not yet implemented.
